How It Works

For audio, the converter decodes your file with the Web Audio API and re-encodes it to the target format (MP3 via LAME, WAV as direct PCM, and OGG/FLAC/AAC/M4A via in-browser FFmpeg). For video, it runs in-browser FFmpeg: a single pass to pull out and encode the audio track, a remux or re-encode for video-to-video, and a two-pass palette pipeline for high-quality GIFs. The whole conversion happens locally — your file never leaves your computer.

Why Convert Files?

Common reasons: pulling an MP3 out of a recorded MP4 for a podcast, making a FLAC archive playable on phones as MP3 or AAC, converting voice memos from M4A to MP3 for platforms that reject M4A, or producing a lossless WAV master from a compressed source for editing. Browser-side conversion means no upload service in the middle.
