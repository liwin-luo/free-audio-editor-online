Remove silence from podcasts, voice memos, and lectures — free, private, no sign-up

SnipSound's Silence Remover scans your recording for stretches of quiet and cuts them out. It's built for the post-recording cleanup that every podcaster, lecturer, and language learner ends up doing: shortening a 45-minute raw take into a 32-minute publishable episode without manually finding and deleting every awkward pause in a DAW.

How the detection actually works

The tool walks through your audio in small windows (about 20 milliseconds each) and computes the loudness of each window in decibels. Any window quieter than your threshold is flagged as silent. Adjacent silent windows merge into regions. A region is only removed if it's longer than minimum silence, and the cut leaves padding seconds on each side so the seam doesn't sound spliced. The defaults are tuned for conversational voice; the sliders let you tighten them for a deadpan podcast or loosen them for a lively interview.

Shortened, not deleted

When you remove silence from audio here, a long pause is shortened rather than cut out altogether. Minimum silence decides how long a pause has to be before it counts — anything shorter stays exactly as recorded, so the natural rhythm of speech survives — and padding keeps a slice of breath at each end of every cut. That is why a cleaned take still sounds like someone talking rather than a stack of spliced words.

Before you download anything you can A/B it: play the original and the cleaned version against each other, with the playhead skipping the removed gaps, and decide from the sound rather than from a number. If you are sending the file on to a paid transcription service, shorter audio is also fewer billable minutes.

Suggested settings

If the defaults aren't matching your material, here are starting points:

-40 / 0.5s / 0.10s
Default (general voice). Works for most podcasts and lectures recorded with a decent microphone in a quiet room.
-35 / 0.4s / 0.08s
Aggressive. Cuts more, including some shorter natural pauses. Use when you specifically want a punchy, fast-paced edit.
-45 / 0.7s / 0.15s
Gentle. Only removes obvious long pauses. Preserves a more natural rhythm. Good for thoughtful interviews and storytelling.
-50 / 1.0s / 0.20s
Quiet rooms. If your recording has very low background noise, you can use a stricter threshold. The longer minimum silence ensures only real pauses get cut.
-30 / 0.5s / 0.10s
Noisy rooms. If there's background hum or fan noise, raise the threshold so quiet speech doesn't get mistaken for silence.

Silence remover FAQ

How does the silence remover decide what's silent?▼
It scans the audio in short windows (about 20 milliseconds each) and measures the loudness of each window in decibels. Any window quieter than your chosen threshold counts as silent. Threshold is dB relative to digital full scale — a real, clearly-quiet voice recording typically sits between -50 dB and -35 dB during pauses, so the default of -40 dB is a sensible starting point.
What's the difference between threshold, minimum silence, and padding?▼
Threshold is the loudness ceiling for "silent" (lower = stricter, fewer regions detected). Minimum silence is how long a quiet stretch has to be before it counts as a real pause worth removing (e.g. 0.5s skips natural inter-word gaps). Padding is how much of the pause to leave intact at each end so the result doesn't sound chopped — a tiny breath on each side prevents the seam from sounding glued together.
Will this destroy the natural rhythm of speech?▼
Only if you set padding too low and minimum silence too short. The defaults (-40 dB / 0.5s min / 0.1s padding) preserve breaths and beats while cutting the long uhh-pauses that bloat a recording. If the output sounds rushed, raise padding to 0.2s or raise minimum silence to 1.0s.
Does it work on music?▼
It can — but you probably want to use the Audio Trimmer instead. Music typically has dynamics and reverb tails that this tool will flag as silence. It's tuned for voice.
Is my file uploaded to a server?▼
No. All silence detection and audio re-encoding happens in your browser using the Web Audio API. Your audio never leaves your device.
What input formats are supported?▼
Any audio format your browser can decode — MP3, WAV, OGG, FLAC, AAC, M4A, and several others. Output formats include MP3 (320, 256, 192, 128 kbps), WAV, FLAC, OGG, AAC and M4A.
How long can my file be?▼
There's no hard limit — files run only as long as your device's memory allows. A typical laptop handles a 60-minute podcast in a few seconds. A 4-hour lecture may take 30+ seconds to decode and process.
