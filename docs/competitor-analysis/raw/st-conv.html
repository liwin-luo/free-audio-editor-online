About This Audio Converter

SoundTools Audio Converter converts between the seven formats that cover virtually every real-world audio need — MP3, FLAC, WAV, AAC, M4A, OGG, and WMA — entirely in your browser. There's no upload, no server, and no wait for a remote queue. You select your file, pick a format, and conversion starts immediately. Powered by FFmpeg, the open-source engine behind most professional audio software, compiled to WebAssembly so it runs entirely in your browser. Your audio never leaves your computer.

The most common use cases: converting FLAC to MP3 for a device that doesn't support lossless playback, converting WMA files you've had sitting around since Windows Media Player days, converting M4A to MP3 for wider compatibility, or converting MP3 to WAV before importing into a DAW. All of these take under 30 seconds.

A note on quality: converting from a lossless format (FLAC or WAV) to a lossy format (MP3, AAC, OGG) will always involve some quality reduction — that's the nature of lossy compression. Conversely, converting from a lossy format to FLAC or WAV produces a larger file but does not recover lost quality. For best results, always keep your original files in FLAC or WAV and convert from those originals rather than from already-compressed copies. When converting to MP3, SoundTools uses 320 kbps — the highest standard bitrate — which is indistinguishable from lossless for most listeners on most playback systems.

Supported Audio Formats

MP3 (MPEG Audio Layer 3)
The most popular lossy audio format. Perfect for music players, phones, and sharing. Excellent balance of file size and quality.

FLAC (Free Lossless Audio Codec)
Lossless compression that preserves perfect audio quality while reducing file size. Ideal for archiving your music collection or audiophile listening.

WAV (Waveform Audio File Format)
Uncompressed audio format with perfect quality. Used in professional audio production and when maximum quality is required.

AAC (Advanced Audio Coding)
Modern lossy format that provides better quality than MP3 at the same bitrate. Used by Apple Music, YouTube, and many streaming services.

M4A (MPEG-4 Audio)
Container format typically using AAC encoding. Common in iTunes and Apple devices.

OGG (Ogg Vorbis)
Open-source lossy format with quality comparable to MP3. Used in gaming and open-source applications.

WMA (Windows Media Audio)
Microsoft's audio format. Can be converted to more universal formats for better compatibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

What audio formats can I convert?

Our converter supports MP3, FLAC, WAV, AAC, M4A, OGG, and WMA. You can convert between any combination of these formats.

Will converting reduce my audio quality?

It depends on the formats. Converting from lossless (FLAC, WAV) to lossy (MP3, AAC, OGG) will reduce quality slightly. Converting between lossy formats may degrade quality. For best results, always keep your original files in a lossless format.

How long does conversion take?

Most files convert in under 30 seconds. Very large files may take up to a minute depending on file size and your internet connection.

Is there a file size limit?

Most audio files up to several hundred MB work perfectly. Extremely large files (1GB+) may take longer to process or may need more RAM.

Do I need to install any software?

No! This converter works instantly with no downloads or installations required. Just visit this page and start converting.

Are my files uploaded to your servers?

No. Everything runs locally in your browser using WebAssembly. Your audio files never leave your device — no upload, no server processing, no cloud storage. This makes conversion faster than server-based tools (no upload/download wait) and completely private.

What bitrate should I use for MP3 conversion?

Our converter uses high-quality default settings (320kbps for MP3, which is the highest quality). This ensures excellent audio quality while keeping reasonable file sizes.

Can I convert multiple files at once?

Currently, the tool processes one file at a time. However, there are no limits on how many files you can convert - just convert them one after another.

Which format should I choose?

For archiving or maximum quality: FLAC or WAV. For everyday listening on phones/computers: MP3 or M4A. For smaller file sizes with good quality: AAC or OGG.

Is this a Zamzar alternative?

Yes. Zamzar is one of the oldest and most trusted online file converters — it supports a huge range of formats well beyond audio. For audio conversion specifically, the free tier limits you to 5 files per day and 50MB per file, and requires uploading your audio to their servers. SoundTools converts audio in your browser with no upload, no file caps, and no daily limits — completely free. If you need to convert an obscure audio format that SoundTools doesn't support, Zamzar's broader format library is the right fallback. For the common formats (MP3, FLAC, WAV, AAC, M4A, OGG), SoundTools is faster and more private.

Is this a CloudConvert alternative?

Yes, for audio conversion. CloudConvert is a powerful cloud-based conversion platform supporting 200+ formats with a polished API — excellent for developers and power users who need bulk or automated conversion. The free tier is limited to 25 conversions per day and uploads files to their servers. SoundTools is narrower in scope (7 audio formats) but converts entirely in your browser with no upload, no account, and no conversion limits.
