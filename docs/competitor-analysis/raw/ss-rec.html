Free online voice recorder with live transcription and a teleprompter

SnipSound's voice recorder records from your microphone in the browser and, when you switch on Captions, writes down your words as you speak. Stop, and the transcript is already there, with times: copy it, or download it as .txt or .srt subtitles. Paste a script instead and it becomes a teleprompter that listens and scrolls at your pace, then shows which words you skipped or changed. There's no software to install, no account, no per-minute fee, and the recording never leaves your device. The speech model behind the captions (about 75 MB) downloads once, then runs on your device. Works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on computers and phones.

How it works (step by step)

  1. Click Start recording. Your browser asks for microphone permission. Click Allow.
  2. Optional: switch on Captions to see your words as you speak, or tap Add a script to read from the teleprompter.
  3. Watch the live waveform and level meter. If the meter never moves, your mic isn't picking up sound — check the input device picker below the recorder.
  4. Pause if you need to. The pause / resume buttons let you take a break without stopping the recording. The result is a single continuous file.
  5. Click Stop. The recording appears with playback, trim and download options, and the transcript if Captions was on.
  6. Pick a format and download. Default is MP3 320 (high quality, broadly compatible). For editing later, pick WAV or FLAC.

How it compares to other voice recorders

Most free online recorders — Online Voice Recorder, Rev, HappyScribe — record in your browser and give you an MP3. Vocaroo is built for sharing: it keeps your recording on its servers so you can send a link. For the words, Rev and HappyScribe send you to their transcription services (Rev's AI transcripts cost $0.25 a minute). SnipSound transcribes while you talk, on your device, at no cost, and adds a teleprompter that follows your voice and a check of your take against your script. What we don't have: share links, cloud storage or speaker labels.

Voice recorder FAQ

How does the voice recorder work?▼
When you click Start, your browser asks for permission to use your microphone. Audio is captured locally via the MediaRecorder API and shown as a live waveform. When you click Stop, the recording stays in your browser — your files never leave your device. You can play it back and download it as MP3, WAV, FLAC, OGG, AAC, or M4A.
Is my voice uploaded to a server?▼
No. Recording, encoding, captions and downloading all happen in your browser. The audio never leaves your device. We can't even access it ourselves.
Is the transcription free?▼
Yes. Captions run on your device with a speech model that downloads once (about 75 MB), so there is no per-minute fee and no minutes quota. On a computer with a graphics card your words appear 1–3 seconds after you say them; on a laptop without one, 6–12 seconds.
Can I use it as a teleprompter?▼
Yes. Tap Add a script, paste your text and press record: after a 3-2-1 countdown the script scrolls at your pace, following your voice. Words you have said dim, and a word you skip gets a dotted underline. Switch to Manual to scroll it yourself. It records audio, not video.
How do I check that I read my script correctly?▼
After the take, script check compares what you said with your script: words you skipped are struck through, words you added are highlighted, and Record again from here restarts at any line you did not read.
Can I turn my recording into subtitles or a captioned video?▼
Yes. Copy the transcript or download it as .txt or .srt subtitles, or make a captioned video where each word appears as it is spoken, then download it as MP4 or open it in the video editor.
What's the maximum recording length?▼
There's no hard limit set by us. Your browser holds the recording in memory as you go, so the practical cap is how much RAM your device has. A typical laptop comfortably records 1–2 hours of voice audio at 48 kHz. Mobile devices may struggle past 30–60 minutes.
Can I pause and resume?▼
Yes — click Pause to suspend the recording, click Resume to keep going. The two segments are stitched into a single output when you click Stop. There's no audible click or gap.
Why does my browser keep asking for microphone permission?▼
Modern browsers require explicit microphone permission for every site, every session, until you mark the permission as "remembered". In Chrome: click the camera icon in the address bar after granting → choose "Always allow". Safari requires you to grant permission per recording by default.
What input formats / devices are supported?▼
Any input device your operating system exposes — built-in microphone, USB mic, headset, audio interface, virtual cable (BlackHole, VB-Cable). If you have multiple devices connected, a picker appears under the recorder so you can choose which one to record from.
What's the output quality?▼
The browser records at 48 kHz, 16-bit mono or stereo (depends on your mic). Default download is 320 kbps MP3 — broadcast-quality for voice. Switch to WAV or FLAC if you plan to do further editing.
